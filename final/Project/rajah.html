Rajah Sulayman Theater

Rajah Sulayman Theater is a cultural center located inside Fort Santiago, Intramuros, Manila. It is named after Rajah Sulayman, a native chieftain who spearheaded resistance against Spanish colonizers in the 16th century. The theater serves as a venue for all forms of performances, from traditional Filipino music, dance, and theater performances. The theater seeks to expose the rich culture of the Philippines while also promoting local artists. It is a local and visitor favorite, with a unique experience that blends the arts and history.

In addition to its cultural significance, the Rajah Sulayman Theater is also a part of history since it is situated within the walls of Fort Santiago, a fort that played a significant role in protecting Manila during the Spanish colonial period. The fact that the theater is situated within the walls of the fort adds to its attraction, as the visitors get a peek into the past while enjoying contemporary performances.

Rizal's Chapel Cell

This main room of the soldiers' school was converted into a chapel-cell for Dr. Jose Rizal, who was transferred here after 56 days of imprisonment at the soldiers' barracks across Plaza de Armas.

It was here that he wrote his last poem, "Mi Último Adiós" (My Last Farewell), which he entrusted to his family and friends for safekeeping. The poem was later published in the newspaper "Kalayaan" (Freedom) on December 30, 1896.
