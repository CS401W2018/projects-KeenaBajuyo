Baluarte de San Francisco Javier

The Baluarte de San Francisco Javier, constructed in 1662, is a renowned bastion and a part of Fort Santiago. It was intended for the fortification against any assault on the fort coming from Manila Bay and was equipped with cannons running along its walls to guard the mouth of the Pasig River. The original protection became extended as a curtain wall when some of it was destroyed in the earthquake of 1645.

It is one of the last remaining buildings from Spanish colonial days and a testament to the architectural prowess of the time. The baluarte is specifically constructed with sturdy walls and a strategic position that offers lovely views of the surrounding terrain.

It was consecrated to St. Francis Xavier, patron of foreign missions, and had seen a number of historical events. It was used as storage rooms until the early 20th century and as cells for Filipino and American soldiers during World War II.

Today, the Baluarte de San Francisco Javier houses several souvenir shops, restaurants, and the Intramuros Visitors' Center.
