Baluarte de San Francisco Javier

Baluarte de San Francisco Javier, constructed in 1662, is a prominent bastion within Fort Santiago. It was built to defend the fort from attacks coming from Manila Bay and was lined with cannons to protect the mouth of the Pasig River. The original fortification was extended as a curtain wall after a section was destroyed during the 1645 earthquake.

It is one of the few remaining structures from the Spanish colonial period and is a testament to the architectural prowess of that era. The baluarte features a unique design, with thick walls and a strategic location that offers stunning views of the surrounding area.

Named after St. Francis Xavier, the patron saint of foreign missions, the baluarte has witnessed numerous historical events. It was used as storage chambers until the early 20th century and served as prison cells for Filipino and American soldiers during World War II.

Today, the Baluarte de San Francisco Javier houses several souvenir shops, food shops, and the Intramuros Visitors’ Center.
