Fort Santiago Gate

This is the entrance to the innermost section of Fort Santiago, which was ruined during the Battle of Manila in 1945. The main gate features a relief carving of Santiago Matamoros (St. James, the "Moor-slayer"), Spain's patron saint, and the coat of arms of the Kingdom of Castile and Leon. The original gate's embossed Spanish soldiers are now defaced.

  • Moor-slayer

The image of St. James, a symbol of Spanish sovereignty, decorates countries once occupied by the Spaniards, such as Chile and Mexico. The gate is a fine example of Spanish colonial architecture, showcasing intricate designs and craftsmanship typical of the period. The destruction of the gate during the Battle of Manila was a seminal moment in the fort's history, symbolizing the intense combat and the eventual liberation of the Philippines from Japanese troops.

Over the years, efforts at the preservation and restoration of parts of the gate and the fort have been undertaken so that individuals may wonder at its cultural and historical value. The gate not only serves as a historical landmark but also as a witness to the resilience and unwavering character of the Filipino people.
