Fort Santiago Gate

Here stands the gate leading to the inner sanctum of Fort Santiago, destroyed during the Battle of Manila in 1945. The main gate features a relief carving of Santiago Matamoros (St. James, the “Moor-slayer”), the patron saint of Spain, alongside the coat of arms of the Kingdom of Castile and Leon. The original gate's embossed Spanish soldiers are now defaced.

The image of St. James, a symbol of Spanish sovereignty, decorates countries once occupied by the Spaniards, such as Chile and Mexico. The gate is a fine example of Spanish colonial architecture, showcasing intricate designs and craftsmanship typical of the period. The destruction of the gate during the Battle of Manila marked a significant moment in the fort's history, symbolizing the intense conflict and the eventual liberation of the Philippines from Japanese occupation.

Over the years, there have been efforts to preserve and restore parts of the gate and the fort, allowing visitors to appreciate its historical and cultural importance. The gate not only serves as a historical landmark but also as a testament to the resilience and enduring spirit of the Filipino people.
